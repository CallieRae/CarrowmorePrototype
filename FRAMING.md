# Frame

**Forty years of logbooks can be translated into an employer’s terms, graded impartially, and used to show a keeper what they are qualified for or the route to get there.**

## Whose problem

**The keeper’s:** *what am I qualified for, what am I nearly qualified for, and what is the shortest route from here to a job?* The employer’s problem runs on the same evidence, so one record serves both views.

## Assumptions

1. Skills are what you know, not how you learned them: “isolated no.2 and earthed off” is what a module assesses.

2. Translation is the value, even where the keeper already knows the skill.

3. Most keepers are nearly qualified: 23 of 38 hold some HV Authorized Person modules; 2 hold the full certificate.

4. Employers will weigh inferred evidence where marked for follow-up. Not testable in mock scenario.

## The Sink

**AI can map logs to employer requirements and word the result plainly enough that a keeper understands and acts on it correctly.** If wrong, or read as a guarantee, it sends a candidate to an interview unqualified.

## Testing

**Method:** On the Authority’s cleaned subset (the question is inference, not OCR repair), ten keepers, chosen by rule with three held out from tuning, were graded blind by hand on five requirements before the AI graded them, citing every log line. No match score: four evidence tiers, and logs never mark an insurer gate as met.

**Kill rules, set before results:** beat keyword search on the held-out keepers, find at least half the real evidence, and be wrong no more than 1 time in 10 to persevere (more than 1 in 4 to kill).

## What wasn’t built

The raw pack (attribution took the Authority three weeks for 40 keepers; at 340 it is the kill at scale), four of six postings, small-boat work (needs the employer’s definition of small boats and swell), appraisals and manager notes (the tool seeks only what it needs), two untested desirables (supervising contractors, mentoring), the age-dependent “younger colleagues” criterion (age is not used), and real authentication (the mock sign-in shows who sees what, with no security).
